## 1. Sequence in production

The human-tested lab rule (`docs/mechlab_sequence.md`), unchanged:
- **The block:** a plain coloured arrow with a small NEXT arrow on a white corner disc.
- **First tap, clear lane:** it launches along its lane and comes back. It sends the usual escape neighbour event: adjacent spinners turn by their rule and adjacent hidden arrows are revealed. Then its NEXT arrow becomes its arrow (stage 2).
- **Second tap:** a normal escape.

**What the first stage is, and is not:**
- **Blocked first tap:** an ordinary blocked tap. It costs a heart, and the stage does not advance.
- **Lane into a shell:** a ram. The shell cracks; the stage does not advance.
- **Not an escape:** locks, gates and switches don't react to it.
- **In Classic:** a productive move like a ram. It is never a mistake, and the chain is kept but earns nothing.

**Engine support:**
- **Undo / Restart:** restore the exact stage and arrow.
- **Solver:** the memo key holds every block's stage.
- **SHOW A MOVE:** can point at a first stage.
- **Hammer:** smashes a Sequence block whole, behind the usual safety check (a solver run with the stages).

## 2. Movable in production

The human-tested lab rule (`docs/mechlab_movable.md`), unchanged:
- **What it is:** a neutral wooden block. No arrow, no colour role, never escapes, and never has to leave: the level is won when every arrow block is gone. It isn't counted in the progress bar.
- **Pushing it:** an arrow whose lane meets it first pushes it exactly one cell. Any direction, any number of times; the arrow stays in its cell.
- **A push fails, as an ordinary blocked tap**, when the cell behind it holds a block, the board edge, another Movable block (no chain pushing) or a blocked portal exit. The message reads "The Movable block can't move there".
- **Through a portal:** it comes out of the partner and lands one cell on.
- **Sequence pusher:** a first-stage Sequence pusher also uses its first stage. A failed push does not.

**Other behaviour:**
- **Tapping it:** free, and explains itself.
- **The Hammer:** never targets it (the design treats it as a board object), and spends nothing.
- **Feel:** the lab's. The arrow dashes, the block slides one cell and settles, a CLUNK plays. Reduced motion gives a plain slide.
- **Undo during the slide:** puts it straight back, so no late callback can move it.

**Solver:**
- **State:** Movable cells are part of the memo key.
- **Search:** cycle-safe. The block can go back and forth, so states on the current path are cut and a state is remembered as lost only when that is proven.
- **Analysis:** `LevelAnalysis` also asks "can this level be won without pushing?" (`push_essential`).

**A rule consequence that shapes every board (from the lab):**
- The last arrow to push a Movable block stays behind it unless it can turn.
- Its way out is a spinner, a Sequence block's next arrow, or a portal carrying the block out of its lane.
- So the Movable lessons (251–254) each have one plain clockwise spinner as the last pusher, as the human-tested lab boards had.

## 5. Design method

`tools/generate_era3.gd` (the Portal-arc method, extended):
- **Spec per slot:** size, block count, the arc's mechanic plus at most two other families (Integration: at most four in all), a difficulty band, and the level's idea as solver-checked needs. For example:
  - a first stage turns a spinner
  - a first stage at the wrong moment loses
  - a push that loses
  - a first-stage push
  - the Movable block travels through a portal
  - a lock's key sits behind the Movable block
- **Search:** boards are found by hill climbing.
- **Acceptance:** a board is accepted only if it passes every campaign rule (`tools/verify_levels.gd`'s own functions), the armor audit and the similarity rule, and is not a weak breather.
- **Milestones:** each must beat every level it must beat by a margin of 2 (difficulty and structural).

**Verifier rules added for 226–300:**
- Sequence only from 226, Movable only from 251.
- Every arc level uses its mechanic.
- Every Third Era mechanic present must matter (except on breathers).
- Lessons: 226–228 and 251–254.
- No stacking.
- 250 / 275 are the hardest of their arcs, and 300 the hardest of the whole Third Era.
- Chapters that introduce a mechanic start their own difficulty curve, like an era.

**Waves, not a staircase:** each Chapter climbs, drops at a breather (235, 244, 258, 264, 270, 281, 287, 294) and climbs again with a new interaction. Breathers are real puzzles: the generator rejects a breather whose Third Era mechanic barely matters.

**Proxies:** the difficulty numbers below are solver proxies. Human tests 1–3 showed they don't predict human difficulty well, so human playtesting decides.

**Reading the flags (reviewed, kept on purpose):**
- **busy (4 families):** the audit counts spinners as a family. Integration (276–300) allows up to four families by design, and none of these boards is over 21 blocks except Level 300 (23, the major milestone).
- **jump:**
  - L255 is the first real Movable puzzle after four lessons.
  - L269 adds Sequence to Movable for the first time.
  - L283 and L296 follow breathers / the curve's wave.
  - None breaks a Chapter's curve, which the verifier checks.
- **restart-heavy:**
  - L284 (Movable x Lock) and L299 are late Expert / Road slots.
  - L300 is the major milestone.
  - Hearts, Undo and SHOW A MOVE are available.
- No Third Era mechanic is decorative: every Sequence block, portal and Movable block in 226–300 passes the verifier's "must matter" rule (breathers included).
